============================
What is Transactive Energy?
============================

Transactive Energy (TE) is an economic and control mechanism that coordinates the generation, consumption, and delivery of electricity across a decentralized network of autonomous agents.

Traditional vs. Transactive Grids
---------------------------------

In traditional grid architectures, power flows unidirectionally from central power plants down to passive end-use consumers. However, modern grids are experiencing high penetrations of **Distributed Energy Resources (DERs)**, including:

* Rooftop and utility-scale Solar Photovoltaic (PV) arrays
* Stationary Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS)
* Electric Vehicle (EV) charging infrastructures
* Flexible building HVAC and water heating loads

Because these assets are distributed, intermittent, and dynamic, top-down command-and-control strategies struggle to scale. Transactive energy replaces rigid centralized control with **price-based market signals**, allowing self-interested or cooperative agents to discover equilibrium schedules that satisfy both local comfort/operational constraints and system-wide grid reliability.

Key Principles of Transactive Energy
------------------------------------

1. **Value Discovery**: Economic value and cleared prices reflect real-time physical scarcity, delivery costs, and asset constraints.
2. **Decentralized Autonomy**: Every asset or facility makes its own decisions based on its local utility/cost function without disclosing sensitive private operational constraints.
3. **Continuous Coordination**: Dynamic multi-interval markets negotiate forward schedules over rolling time horizons (e.g., 24-hour lookaheads cleared every 15 or 60 minutes).
4. **Physical Feasibility**: Cleared economic transactions translate directly into physical commands (actuations) dispatched to hardware or simulation models.
